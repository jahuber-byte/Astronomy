What to watch

1. Follow sunriseThe observer rotates into the illuminated half of Earth as the Sun crosses the eastern horizon.
2. Compare the MoonAt the same clock time one day later, the Moon has moved east relative to the Sun and stars.
3. Earth catches upThe observer must rotate a little farther before the Moon reaches the eastern horizon again—about 50 minutes later.

Quick challenge

Model limits: Sizes and distances are greatly exaggerated. The lunar orbit is drawn in the same plane as the diagram so rise-time geometry is easy to see. In reality the Moon's orbit is tilted by about 5° to the ecliptic, so the Moon usually passes above or below Earth's shadow; a visual crossing of the flattened shadow cone in Pane 1 should not be interpreted as predicting an eclipse. The “about 50 minutes later” rule is an average.
