Investigation 1

What happens when an atom becomes an ion?

Select an element and change its number of electrons. Observe the charge, the number of occupied shells, and how its size changes. Try at least four different transformations, including both a cation (positive ion) and an anion (negative ion).

The number of protons stays the same when an atom forms an ion. Electrons are lost or gained. The diagrams are simplified shell models, not pictures of electron orbits; the outer shell of anions is drawn slightly larger to suggest increased electron repulsion, not to represent a measured radius.

Investigation 2

How does ion formation change atomic size?

Compare the approximate measured radii of neutral atoms and their common ions. The circle diameters scale with the listed radii. Try at least four pairs, including at least one cation (positive ion) and one anion (negative ion).

Bonus discovery: same number of electrons, different size

These ions all have 10 electrons, but different numbers of protons. More protons pull on the same number of electrons more strongly, making the ion smaller.
