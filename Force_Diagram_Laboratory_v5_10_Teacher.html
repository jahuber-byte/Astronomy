How this laboratory works

Progressive hybrid: The first scenarios guide you toward the forces you need. Later scenarios expect more independent diagram construction. Each challenge follows three steps: identify forces, construct the force diagram, and explain what it means.

Force diagram conventions: Represent the chosen object as a particle. Draw only forces on that object. Every actual force starts at the particle. Gravity points vertically downward, even on a hill. Motion and velocity are not forces. Equal forces have equal arrow lengths; forces do not have to be equal simply because the object is moving.

Directions: 0° = right, 90° = up, 180° = left, 270° (or −90°) = down. The arrow-length scale is qualitative unless a problem explicitly provides numbers. Equilibrium answers are accepted when the net-force magnitude is within about one 5-unit slider step of zero; individual force lengths do not need to match a single model solution.

Feedback: The first incorrect attempt gives a hint. Further attempts provide more specific guidance. The Reveal button explains the correct model. In Teacher mode, you can freely navigate and reveal solutions.

Scope: This first version emphasizes single-object force diagrams, equilibrium, and elementary component reasoning. The angle-and-friction lab adds quantitative relationships. Multi-cable statics and full inclined-plane calculations are candidates for a later version.

Important modeling assumptions: Ignore air resistance unless the problem mentions it. For constant-velocity cases, the net force is zero. For projectiles after release, there is no continuing “force of the throw.”
