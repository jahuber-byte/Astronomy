From full electron configurations to noble-gas shorthand · Introductory chemistry

Start here: one configuration, two ways

A noble-gas configuration shortens a full electron configuration. The bracketed noble gas stands for all the electrons in that gas. It does not change where the electrons are.

Example: Sodium (Na), 11 electrons
Full: 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s¹
Shorthand: [Ne] 3s¹
Neon represents the first 10 electrons. The remaining 1 electron is 3s¹.
Expand [Ne]: where did the electrons go?

[Ne] = 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ = 10 electrons. Therefore [Ne] 3s¹ and 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s¹ describe the same atom.

Rule: choose the noble gas with the largest atomic number smaller than your element's atomic number. Then write the electrons that come after it.

Find the Noble Gas

Question 1

Which noble gas comes immediately before this element?

Choose the noble-gas core, then build the remaining orbitals. Add one orbital at a time.

Type the entire noble-gas configuration. For example: [Ne] 3s2 3p5.

Spaces, ^ exponents, and superscripts are accepted. Use correct capitalization.

Periodic-table reference

Teal = noble gas · Gold = current element. Atomic numbers increase left to right and top to bottom.

Noble gases: He 2 · Ne 10 · Ar 18 · Kr 36

Helpful reminders

1. Find the preceding noble gas.

2. Put its symbol in square brackets.

3. Add the remaining orbitals and their electron counts.

4. Check that the total number of electrons equals the atomic number.

How do I count electrons?

Add the noble gas's atomic number to the superscripts after the brackets. Example: [Ne] 3s² 3p⁴ has 10 + 2 + 4 = 16 electrons.
