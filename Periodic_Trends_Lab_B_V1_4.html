Investigation 2

Which elements behave like metals?

Metallic character describes how strongly an element exhibits typical metallic properties, including a tendency to lose electrons and form positive ions. Explore the whole periodic table to identify metals, metalloids, and nonmetals. The center contains transition metals, and the two detached rows contain inner transition metals. For numerical comparisons of valence electrons and ionization energy, use the available reference elements through krypton. You can use main-group elements from all seven periods to satisfy the across-period and down-group exploration requirements. Try at least five distinct pairs, including one across a period, one down a group, and one comparing a metal with a nonmetal.

Scientific note: Metallic character is a qualitative trend, not a single universally measured number. The first ionization energy shown here is supporting evidence—not a numerical metallic-character score. Metalloids show mixed properties. Transition metals are metals: many conduct heat and electricity, are malleable, and form positive ions, but their d-electron configurations make simple left-to-right trend predictions less reliable. The across-period and down-group requirements use main-group pairs from any period, including elements below krypton; other pairs count toward the five total. Numerical first-ionization-energy values are shown only for the original reference elements through krypton. Classifications are simplified.
