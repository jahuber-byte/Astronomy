2. Observer view — Northern Hemisphere orientation

This is a teaching model of the zodiac band, not a full planetarium view. East is on the left and west is on the right, matching the view of a Northern Hemisphere observer looking generally south along the zodiac. The center is the direction crossing the local meridian at the selected time.

What to notice

The key idea: constellations near the Sun's direction are washed out by daylight, while constellations roughly opposite the Sun are best placed for the middle of the night.

Quick challenge

Model limits: This first version intentionally simplifies the sky to the ecliptic/zodiac band. It does not yet calculate exact altitude, azimuth, latitude effects, atmospheric twilight, or the detailed shape of constellation boundaries. The date ranges used for the Sun's astronomical zodiac constellations are approximate. The left-hand orbit is shown from above the north side of the ecliptic, so Earth revolves counterclockwise. The zodiac ring is schematic and is not drawn to scale; the stars are vastly farther away than Earth's orbit.
